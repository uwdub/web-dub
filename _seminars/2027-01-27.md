title:      "Twenty-Five Years, Five Projects, and Five Reflections on Accessible Computing"

location:   "Kane 225 - Hybrid via Zoom"

abstract: |
  I began working on accessible computing in 2001 at the start of my doctoral program by developing word prediction software for Palm PDA devices. Today, 25 years later, accessibility has remained a core focus of my work and the work of my students in the field of human-computer interaction (HCI). In this talk, I will share five projects that have significantly shaped my work, along with five reflections that continue to inform my perspective. My goal is to convey ideas from which I would have benefitted 25 years ago as a junior researcher starting out in accessible computing. My five projects are EdgeWrite, Slide Rule, enhanced area cursors and, in particular, the Pointing Magnifier, ability-based design, and the epidemiological model of mobile app accessibility. My five reflections concern prioritizing depth, the power of participant-driven prototyping, the opportunities and obligations of field deployments, the importance of conceptual contributions, and the value of cross-disciplinary deep-dives. I conclude by addressing some of the promises and pitfalls of artificial intelligence for accessibility, drawing on these reflections to light our way.

bio: |
  Jacob O. Wobbrock is a Professor of Information and, by courtesy, of Computer Science & Engineering at the University of Washington (UW). He directs the ACE Lab and is an Associate Director and former founding Co-Director of the UW Center for Research and Education on Accessible Technology and Experiences (CREATE). Since 2001, his research has pursued accessible interaction techniques, adaptive user interfaces, mobile accessibility, and human-centered artificial intelligence. Wobbrock received the 2017 SIGCHI Social Impact Award, was inducted into the CHI Academy in 2019, and was named an ACM Fellow in 2021 “for contributions to human-computer interaction and accessible computing.”
